## 2. What Went Well

1. **The #1 ship risk is engineering-retired.** Determinism is provable, not hoped-for: server-side quorum + HALT is live-verified (a 3-window F9 loopback run HALTed both clients), the cross-platform Windows↔Linux golden gate is green via WSL Ubuntu, and the CI golden gate fails the build on any checksum diff on every push.
2. **The determinism floor never regressed while the engine was rebuilt.** Byte-identical-golden discipline ("never re-record, fix the leak") held flawlessly across all 19 stories. The only golden re-records were the four *intentional* `AlgoVersion` bumps (1.3b, 1.5, 1.12, 1.13) plus one new golden (1.8b). No golden was ever hand-edited.
3. **The MainScene strangle was clean.** The 2,234-LOC god-object came down to 1,007 LOC via the 1.8a/b/c split — each slice golden-gated and independently shippable. 1.8a landed with zero patches; the whole sim spine is now Godot-free and reused verbatim by the server.
4. **The 3-layer adversarial review earned its budget.** It caught the headline defect in nearly every wired story: the silent production server at `MainScene.cs:227` (1.9b), spectator-quorum pollution (1.9a), float↔string bans that looked wired but caught nothing (1.10b), and the spawn-path gap (1.13). The Edge Case Hunter layer was the consistent MVP.
5. **Verification stories paid for themselves.** 1.11 ("just smoke-test four systems") uncovered two real gaps — the LLM-trigger validation was entirely unenforced, and the AI map generator had zero RNG — and produced a real deterministic `ProceduralMapGenerator` (hash `0xB46313CA`).
6. **A load-bearing pre-existing bug was found and fixed.** The 1.9a ENet `peer.GetPacket()` fix corrected a transport layer that was silently dropping *every* data packet — unblocking real multiplayer broadly.
7. **Scope discipline held under pressure.** Mid-flight scope changes (multi-waypoint patrol, build-a-generator, the root-cause spawn refactor) were absorbed without slipping, and parked items were tracked honestly in `deferred-work.md` with named owners rather than silently dropped.

## 3. What Was Hard — Patterns & Growth Areas

1. **Recurring defect class: new per-entity state not propagated through *every* path.** 1.12's "zombie patrol route" (route not reset on leaving Patrol) and 1.13's new SoA fields wired into only 1 of 5 spawn paths are the *same shape*. Notably, 1.13's story-creation pre-warned about the 1.12 lesson and a sibling variant still slipped through. **The durable fix is structural — one `OrderApplier`, one `ApplyUnitDefinition` mapper — not "remember to update both."** → Action item A2.
2. **"Green proof masks a real gap."** The loopback self-test passed only because it injected its own log sink while the real server dropped the verdict (1.9b); RS0030 bans matched a micro-test but caught nothing in the real build (1.10b). **Lesson internalized: build it, inject a real violation, observe the failure — don't reason from assumed semantics.** → Action item A3.
3. **Latent bugs surface only at real integration.** The ENet packet-drop, a headless NRE, and an early-Ready deadlock all "compiled and tested green." The real risk in the netcode stories was untested glue/transport, not algorithms.
4. **Toolchain friction outweighed code friction on the infra stories.** `--no-incremental` (release-gate false pass), `--` illegal in XML comments, editorconfig-for-*linked*-sources, PowerShell→WSL→bash quoting, CS0579 from a shared `obj/`, WSL SDK-band/sudo/PATH. (Captured as optional follow-up A4.)
5. **"No tautological assert."** Born in 1.1, re-flagged nearly every story — the single most durable review lesson. Tests must assert observable outcomes against independently-derived expected values.
6. **Scope-fence discipline was the hardest human factor.** Every foundation story explicitly fought the urge to "finish the job" early; stated repeatedly as "the single hardest thing is discipline."
7. **Minor carried debt:** CS8632 nullable warnings rode untouched the whole epic; AI float→Fixed (D2) is the one strategic deferral that will matter for AI-in-lockstep and cross-platform AI parity.
