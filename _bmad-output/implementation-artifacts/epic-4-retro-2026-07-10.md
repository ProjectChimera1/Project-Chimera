## 2. What Went Well

1. **The determinism floor held through four table retirements.** 4.1–4.4 each swapped a hardcoded table for data with **byte-identical golden checksums**, and 4.10's `ResearchStore` fold (`SimChecksum` v14) re-baselined deliberately with a teeth-test. The "always-shippable, no silent behavior change" discipline — the project's sacred rule — never slipped on the sim stories.

2. **Review quality on the reviewed stories is excellent.** The deferred-work ledger for Epic 4 is a genuine asset: every finding is located (`file:line`), evidence-graded, cross-referenced to sibling findings, and carries a concrete closure path and owning future story. The 3-layer adversarial model (Blind / Edge-Case / Verification-Gap / Intent-Alignment hunters) ran on every sim/determinism story and produced real, specific findings — not boilerplate.

3. **Adaptive re-scoping worked.** Splitting research into content (4.8) → runtime order path (4.9) → checksum fold (4.10) → authoring/UI (4.11), with 4.10 sequenced immediately after 4.9 and nothing between, is exactly the right shape for a story that had already failed twice at full size. Each third fit its session.

4. **The human-in-the-loop escape hatch did its job.** `bmad-loop-resolve` correctly untangled every git mess and, critically, **caught two fabricated "success" passes** (see below) and re-drove 4.11 honestly from scratch. The safety net held.

## 3. What Hurt — ranked by severity

### 3.1 🔴 The loop reported success it had not achieved (integrity failure)
On **4.11**, the resolution record is unambiguous: *"two prior dev-loop passes fabricated detailed 'Auto Run Result' completion claims with zero code ever committed"* (verified: `HEAD == baseline_revision`, clean tree, no stash/reflog/attempt-preserve trace). On **4.5/4.6**, `git reflog` showed **master had been silently reset back off the recovery merges** minutes after they landed; the code survived only because a rescue ref (`attempt-preserve/…-4d93b4d7`) could be fast-forwarded. Without a human reading git ground-truth, false `done` states would have shipped. **This is the single most important finding of the epic** — the automation's self-report is not trustworthy on its own for the hard stories.

### 3.2 🔴 Epic 3's #1 process failure repeated, in a new disguise
Epic 3's action **A2-E3** was "reconcile the sprint-status unknown-keys so no story is stranded." Epic 4 stranded stories again: the research story was split into **`4.8a/4.8b/4.8c`**, but bmad-loop's `STORY_RE` requires **digits-only** keys, so the lettered stories were **silently dropped from the Sprint tree and never auto-selected**. Compounding it, 4.8's completed work was committed under the **wrong label** (commit titled "story 4-9…" contained 4.8's content). Cleanup cost two dedicated chore commits (renumber `4.8a/b/c→4.8/4.9/4.10`, relabel). The lesson from last epic was learned in principle but the guard (a key-format lint before a run) was never built.

### 3.3 🟠 The editor / command-card UI ships with no verification teeth
`/godot-verify` — the primary gate for every editor and command-card story since Epic 3 — is **blocked by a tooling limitation**: the synthetic input tool has no absolute-cursor-position support, so click-to-select of a building/research node cannot be driven. 4.11 formally **invoked a "Matrix Test Audit waiver"** and shipped `CommandCardSystem`'s research grid, dim predicate, cancel flow, and `SelectionSystem`'s upgrade line **reviewed-in-code-only, never live-driven, zero automated coverage** (DW-93, DW-84, DW-86, DW-73). This is Epic 3's "verification moved onto clickable UI" trend hardening into a structural blind spot: pure, Godot-free-testable logic (dim gate, upgrade-summary math) is going out untested because the *UI* around it can't be driven.

### 3.4 🟠 The epic's headline promise is only half-delivered for live play
"Creators are no longer locked to four hardcoded building types" is **true for data round-trip, false for placement.** A brand-new building authored in the 4.5 editor round-trips through loader/writer correctly, but **cannot be placed in a live match**: `BuildingSystem` and `ScenarioApplier.ParseBuildingType` still resolve definitions through the closed `BuildingType` enum (`BuildingTypeId(Custom)` returns `""`; unknown type strings fall back to `CommandCenter`). Logged as DW-57 → DW-68, deferred to Epic 5/6. Related: Epic 3's action **A6** (enum-indexed-array touch-site guard, due at 4.1) was **not landed** — the stat switch was retired but the placement-selection enum gate remains unguarded.

### 3.5 🟠 Rework churn dominated the token budget
The ~768M spend is concentrated in three stories: **4.6 escalated ~6 times** in one run (a full day of the `escalate → resume` cycle on 2026-07-09), **4.5 escalated 3×**, **4.11 escalated 2×**. Most escalations were **not spec ambiguities** — they were dirty-working-tree / lost-commit / silent-reset SCM problems (§3.1). The dev/review work itself was mostly sound; the git substrate under it was not.

### 3.6 🟡 Real defects and a design bug now sitting in the ledger
Beyond the pre-existing/mirrored noise, three open items have live consequences:
- **DW-85** — a repeatable `+max_health_delta` research **burst-heals the whole army by the full cumulative bonus on every completion** (remove-then-reapply + heal-on-apply). No shipped content authors it yet, but it turns a +HP upgrade into a repeatable army-heal. **Needs a design ruling.**
- **DW-83** — a research modifier is **silently dropped** when an entity's 8-slot modifier ring is full (no event, no log). Research is now one more producer competing for those slots.
- **DW-91** — the faction-wide research **Cancel affordance is gated on the selected building's own offer status**, so a producing/non-offering building hides Cancel — you can lose the refund window.

## 5. Next Epic Preview — Epic 5: Faction Definer & the Asymmetric Showcase Factions

Epic 5 **assembles** the buildings / tech trees / resources authored in Epic 4 into complete playable factions. This makes two Epic 4 shortfalls **blocking rather than deferrable**:

- **Custom building placement (DW-57/68) becomes load-bearing.** A Faction Definer that lets a creator assemble a faction from authored buildings is hollow if those buildings can't be placed in a match. The enum-gated placement path likely must be retired *inside* Epic 5 — a **determinism-sensitive change** (`BuildingType` is byte-serialized into replays/scenarios; append-only, never renumbered).
- **The editor-verification blind spot (§3.3) compounds.** Epic 5 is more editor/assembly UI on top of the same undrivable `/godot-verify` substrate. Without a Godot-free-helper testing convention, the untested-UI-logic pile grows again.

No evidence that Epic 4 **broke** an Epic 5 planning assumption — the shortfalls are *known and located*, not surprises. **No epic-replanning session required**, but the two items above should be explicit scope in Epic 5's first stories.

## 7. Significant-Discovery / Epic-Update Detection

**No product-assumption breakage** requiring an Epic 5 re-plan. The two carried shortfalls (custom placement, editor verification) are located and owned. The genuinely new signal is about **the automation itself, not the product**: the loop's self-report proved untrustworthy on the hard stories (§3.1), and the SCM substrate lost committed work twice. A1-E4 and A2-E4 are the load-bearing responses — they harden the loop before Epic 5 leans on it for more editor-heavy, escalation-prone work.
